How much water to highlight
Measures how fast the layer changes across distance and paints the fastest‑changing water. The slider sets how much of what you can see gets highlighted, so it finds the strongest edges on screen whatever the layer or season.

Wind & current arrows

Both are forecast model output, and both follow the timeline scrubber above the map buttons — drag it to see wind and current change through the day. Current is a broad‑scale ocean model. It does not show the tidal stream, which is what actually moves the water inshore of Broome on a big tide — read it as background drift, not the run. For that, use the Tide tab, which does capture Broome's real tidal swing.

Marine cartography

Seamarks come from OpenSeaMap, which is crowd‑sourced. Useful for orientation, not a substitute for the charts on your plotter. No soundings you should trust, no hazard data, no official survey.

Where the data comes from

  • Chlorophyll‑a — VIIRS on NOAA‑21, via NASA GIBS. Daily, roughly 1 km.
  • Sea surface temperature — GHRSST Level 4 MUR, via NASA GIBS. Daily blended analysis.
  • Bathymetry — Esri Ocean Basemap and GEBCO. Seamarks — OpenSeaMap.
  • Wind, swell, current — Open‑Meteo forecast and marine models.
  • Tide — harmonic constituents from the Neaps tide database (TICON‑4), computed on the phone.

Cloud blanks out the satellite layers. If today looks empty, wind the date back a day or two. Imagery is an aid, not a navigation instrument.
